# ShiftMate — one-page summary

## Point of view
ShiftMate is an instrument that belongs in the cab: calm and quiet until a person is at risk, then direct and hard to miss, and always honest about what it knows. It looks out for the operator the way a good banksman does, speaking plainly, never keeping score, and handing the judgement back to the expert in the seat.

## The signature element: the Reach
A plan-view gauge of the machine's own working envelope: swing radius (heavy), reach + 2 m (solid), 2× reach (dashed like a string line), with the boom showing the current swing. A person appears as a dot in the band they occupy, and that band fills with its tier colour. Inside the swing radius, the band is hatched. The same geometry runs from the status rail (84 / 148 px) to the Safety Guard panel, fills the left half of the P1 takeover (460 px), and draws every machine's rings on the supervisor's live map. It is how operators already think about space, so there is nothing new to learn. Everything around it is kept quiet: graphite, chalk, concrete, and one type family.

## Three things I deliberately did not do
1. **No brand colour.** Red, orange, yellow, green and blue already have meanings on a jobsite, and ANSI's purple marks radiation (nuclear density gauges are used on roadworks). Any hue ShiftMate claimed would eventually be read as a warning, or would make a real warning look ordinary. The primary action is graphite, and the identity comes from the Reach and Anek's wide decal cut.
2. **No KPI tiles, scores or rankings of operators.** The supervisor summary opens with one sentence and one lead number about *causes* ("3 h 20 m waiting for trucks"), and idle time is attributed to trucks, warm-up or "not sure" rather than to people. Ravi would ignore, and resent, a tool that grades him; supervisors get more from knowing that one more truck would fix the problem.
3. **No decoration borrowed from hazards.** No caution-tape borders, no hatched headers, no red for "delete", no green for "start". The diagonal hatch appears only when a person is inside the swing radius, so when an operator sees it, it means exactly that.

Where the brief conflicted with itself, I chose safety and legibility. The "danger" proximity tier is orange because its action is to slow down, and red is kept for stop. Night mode keeps secondary text at 4.5–5.8:1 to protect dark adaptation. The layouts assume the brief's reference density and ask smaller tablets to scale up rather than shrink targets.
